How the levels work

  • The levels go all the way through 2nd grade: 143 levels in 16 games. She moves up only after she has really mastered a level.
  • Mastered means 9 of her last 10 right on the first try, with at least 8 tries at that level. If she mastered a level once before, 5 in a row wins it back.
  • Say It Fast only counts a number when she says it within about 3 seconds (4 seconds for big numbers). Kindergarten screeners time this skill.
  • Every 4th question is review from a level she already passed. If she misses 2 of her last 3 review questions from a level, the game sends her back to that level to practice it again.
  • If she misses about half of her first 8 tries on a new level, she steps back one level to build it up.
  • You can move any game up or down with the − and + buttons below. Moving down clears the levels above it, so she earns them again. On each game's level map she can also replay any level she has passed.

Recent changes

    Where kindergarten needs to end up (TN standards)

    • Count to 100 by ones and tens. Count on from any number.
    • Read and write numbers 0–20.
    • Count up to 20 objects and tell how many. The last number said is the total.
    • Compare groups and numbers up to 10: more, fewer, same.
    • Add and subtract within 10. Fluent within 5. Know the pairs that make 10.
    • Teen numbers are ten and some ones (14 = 10 + 4).
    • Reading: all letter names and sounds, rhymes, first and last sounds, sight words, and sounding out words like cat and sun.

    By the winter aimsweb check (January): she should name any number 0–20 instantly, see 1–5 dots without counting, count objects to 10 with no mistakes, and put two small dot groups together. After kindergarten the games keep going into 1st and 2nd grade: adding and subtracting to 1,000, place value, telling time, shapes and fractions, blends, magic e, vowel teams, and reading sentences.

    A simple routine

    • 5 days a week, 10–15 minutes. One Today's Adventure, plus 3 minutes of Say It Fast with you. That game is played out loud.
    • Egg carton ten frame: cut two cups off a dozen carton. Fill it with snacks: "show me 7." Ask "how many empty?"
    • Dice roll: roll two dice and say the total fast, no counting dots one at a time.
    • Number hunt 0–20: house numbers, prices, the oven clock, license plates in the car.
    • Card game War with cards A–10: whoever has more wins. This is comparing.
    • Count the stairs out loud, then count backward on the way down.
